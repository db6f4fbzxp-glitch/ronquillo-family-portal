Case Law

Foundational Equity Cases

Grupo Mexicano de Desarrollo v. Alliance Bond Fund, 527 U.S. 308 (1999)
Equity jurisdiction of federal courts is coextensive with the High Court of Chancery in England at the time of the Constitution and the Judiciary Act of 1789. THE foundational authority for all equitable remedies.
Supreme Court All Cha0s Files
Porter v. Warner Holding Co., 328 U.S. 395 (1946)
"Unless a statute in so many words, or by a necessary and inescapable inference, restricts the court's jurisdiction in equity, the full scope of that jurisdiction is to be recognized and applied." Presumption favoring equity.
Supreme Court Multiple Files
Russell v. Todd, 309 U.S. 280 (1940)
Equity accounting jurisdiction exists even where an action at law is available. The jurisdiction is "well established" and "not defeated" by legal alternatives.
Supreme Court Multiple Files
Hecht Co. v. Bowles, 321 U.S. 321 (1944)
Equity moulds each decree to the necessities of the particular case. Flexibility over rigidity in equitable remedies.
Supreme Court
Deckert v. Independence Shares Corp., 311 U.S. 282 (1940)
Court has jurisdiction to preserve status quo by interlocutory injunction pending final hearing. Foundation for quia timet injunction authority.
Supreme Court Multiple Files
CIGNA Corp. v. Amara, 563 U.S. 421 (2011)
Surcharge, constructive trust, and accounting are traditional equitable remedies. Modern confirmation that federal courts sitting in equity have authority to provide these remedies.
Supreme Court Multiple Files
Dairy Queen, Inc. v. Wood, 369 U.S. 469 (1962)
Where equitable accounting is the substantive relief sought (not merely incidental), no jury trial right exists. The matter rests in equity, not at law.
Supreme Court
Shields v. Barrow, 58 U.S. (17 How.) 130 (1854)
Defines indispensable parties: persons with interest of such nature that a final decree cannot be made without affecting that interest, or leaving the controversy inconsistent with equity and good conscience.
Supreme Court Multiple Files

Federal Jurisdiction & Immunity

Ex parte Young, 209 U.S. 123 (1908)
Suit for prospective injunctive relief against state officer in official capacity is not a suit against the state. Eleventh Amendment does not bar. Gateway doctrine for equitable accounting.
Supreme Court Multiple Files
Exxon Mobil Corp. v. Saudi Basic Industries, 544 U.S. 280 (2005)
Rooker-Feldman bars review only when federal claim is "inextricably intertwined" with state judgment. Narrowed the doctrine. Fund accounting is not intertwined with underlying judgment.
Supreme Court Court Accounting
Younger v. Harris, 401 U.S. 37 (1971)
Federal courts abstain from interfering with ongoing state proceedings. Does NOT bar federal action targeting custodial handling when proceeding has concluded.
Supreme Court Court Accounting
Forrester v. White, 484 U.S. 219 (1988)
Judicial immunity protects judicial acts, not administrative/ministerial ones. Fund custody is ministerial, not judicial. No immunity for clerk's accounting duty.
Supreme Court Court Accounting
Antoine v. Byers & Anderson, 508 U.S. 429 (1993)
Quasi-judicial immunity limited to acts "integral to judicial process." Fund custody is not integral. No immunity shield for fund custodians.

Equitable Maxims & Doctrines

The timeless principles of conscience that govern all equity proceedings.

The Five Maxims

"Equity will not suffer a wrong to be without a remedy."
Gibson, Suits in Chancery § 2
The foundational rationale for equity jurisdiction itself. If a wrong exists and law cannot remedy it, equity steps in.
"He who seeks equity must do equity."
Pomeroy, Equity Jurisprudence § 385
Reciprocal duty analysis. Petitioner must plead clean hands and demonstrate good faith.
"Equity acts in personam."
Pomeroy, Equity Jurisprudence § 428
Justifies in personam injunctions directly against custodians and respondents. Equity commands the person, not the property.
"Equity regards that as done which ought to be done."
Story, Equity Jurisprudence § 64G
Constructive trust rationale and specific performance foundation. What should have been done is treated as already done.
"Where one of two innocent persons must suffer, he through whose agency the loss occurred must bear it."
Story, Equity Jurisprudence § 64
Loss allocation doctrine. Shifts the burden to the custodian whose actions or inaction caused the loss.

Key Doctrines

Doctrine of Substance Over Form
The actual nature of the action (equitable accounting) is not altered by compliance with Federal Rules procedural form. Rule 2 merger changed the form, not the substance. Authority: Grupo Mexicano, Porter v. Warner, Guaranty Trust v. York.
Doctrine
Doctrine of Exclusive Record Control
Pomeroy § 1421, Third Independent Basis: When the respondent holds vouchers, records, and books necessary to state the account, and petitioner cannot obtain discovery by other means, equity jurisdiction arises independently. This is the STRONGEST basis — sufficient alone.
Doctrine
Fiduciary Accounting Doctrine
Elements: (1) One party holds another's funds/property, (2) Trust and confidence relationship, (3) Duty to account. Remedies: surcharge, constructive trust, equitable tracing, restoration.
Doctrine
Double Recovery Bar
A creditor cannot collect the same debt twice. Applies to GAP insurance + deficiency claims, overpayment recoupment + offset intercepts, and multiple assessments. Remedy: surcharge to reduce over-collected amount.
Doctrine
Adequate Remedy at Law Test
Inadequacy established when: (1) Statutory remedy produces documents, not sworn accounting, (2) Cannot subject entries to surcharge and falsification, (3) Cannot impose constructive trust or equitable tracing, (4) Structural barrier prevents specific relief sought.
Doctrine

Restatement (Third) of Restitution & Unjust Enrichment

The American Law Institute's definitive authority on restitution, constructive trusts, property (mortgages), trusts, and equitable remedies. 22 sections mapped to our case across three Restatement volumes. Bluebook cite: Restatement (Third) of [Subject] § ___ (Am. L. Inst.).

TIER 1 — Cite in Every Filing

Restatement Tier 1

§ 1. Restitution and Unjust Enrichment

Rule: “A person who is unjustly enriched at the expense of another is subject to liability in restitution.”

Application: Defendants collected mortgage payments, servicing fees, and securitization proceeds without valid legal authority. Liability is independent of fault, contract, or wrongdoing.

Restatement Tier 1

§ 3. Wrongful Gain

Rule: “A person is not permitted to profit by his own wrong.” Restitution may exceed claimant's loss to strip wrongful profits.

Application: All servicing fees, late charges, force-placed insurance, and securitization proceeds must be disgorged.

Restatement Tier 1

§ 4. No Inadequacy Requirement

Rule: “A claimant otherwise entitled to a remedy for unjust enrichment, including a remedy originating in equity, need not demonstrate the inadequacy of available remedies at law.”

Application: CRITICAL for NJ Chancery. Equitable remedies available without showing money damages inadequate. Eliminates a common defense.

Restatement Tier 1

§ 13. Fraud and Misrepresentation

Rule: A transfer induced by fraud is subject to rescission. Where transferor had no knowledge or opportunity to learn the character of the transfer, it is void—not merely voidable.

Application: MERS assignments by unauthorized robo-signers are VOID ab initio. No subsequent transferee acquired any rights.

Restatement Tier 1

§ 17. Lack of Authority — CORNERSTONE

Rule: “A transfer by an agent, trustee, or other fiduciary outside the scope of the transferor's authority . . . is subject to rescission and restitution.”

Application: MERS had no authority to assign beneficial interest. Robo-signers had no authority to execute. Servicers lacked valid assignment. Every unauthorized transfer is avoidable.

Restatement Tier 1

§ 43. Fiduciary Breach — Prophylactic Disgorgement

Rule: A person who obtains a benefit in breach of fiduciary duty is liable in restitution. Disgorgement is prophylactic—no proof of injury required. Extends to third parties who benefit from the breach.

Application: CNB/CrossCountry (origination), Lakeview (servicing/escrow), MERS (nominee duties). Must disgorge ALL consequential gains.

Restatement Tier 1

§ 51. Disgorgement — Conscious Wrongdoer Standard

Rule: “Conscious wrongdoer” = acts despite knowledge or known risk of violating others' rights. Burden of proof on DEFENDANT for apportionment. Residual uncertainty resolved AGAINST wrongdoer.

Application: All defendants qualify. Must disgorge all servicing fees, late charges, force-placed insurance, securitization proceeds, guarantee fees.

Restatement Tier 1

§ 55. Constructive Trust — Priority Over Creditors

Rule: Defendant unjustly enriched by acquiring title to identifiable property may be declared constructive trustee. Gives priority over general creditors. Not subject to recording statutes—arises by operation of law.

Application: Any entity claiming rights through the broken chain holds mere legal title subject to the family's equitable ownership.

Restatement Tier 1

§ 60. Priority Over Lien Creditors

Rule: Restitution right from identifiable property is superior to competing creditors. Judicial liens do NOT make holder a purchaser. Constructive trusts retain priority over recorded interests.

Application: Family's equitable interest defeats all defendant liens, recorded assignments, and creditor claims.

Restatement Tier 1 KILL SHOT

R3d Property (Mortgages) § 5.4(c) — Entitled Person Rule

Rule: “A mortgage may be enforced only by, or in behalf of, a person who is entitled to enforce the obligation the mortgage secures.”

Application: THE KILL SHOT. Lakeview cannot prove entitlement to the note. UCC § 3-203 requires PHYSICAL DELIVERY — no delivery documented. Third Circuit (DLJ Mortgage Capital) requires POSSESSION + INDORSEMENT — Lakeview has neither. Illustration 7: Mortgage without note = CANNOT FORECLOSE. Period.

Bluebook: Restatement (Third) of Property (Mortgages) § 5.4(c) (Am. L. Inst. 1997).

Restatement Tier 1 SHIELD

R3d Trusts § 58 Cmt d(2) — Spendthrift Protection

Rule: Beneficial interests in a spendthrift trust “cannot be attached by judgment creditors” and do “not become an asset of the beneficiary’s bankruptcy estate.” Confirmed by 11 U.S.C. § 541(c)(2).

Application: Minor sons’ beneficial interests in the Express Trust are UNREACHABLE. No creditor, no foreclosure, no bankruptcy can touch the trust res. Must restrain BOTH voluntary AND involuntary alienation (Comment b(2)).

Bluebook: Restatement (Third) of Trusts § 58 cmt. d(2) (Am. L. Inst. 2003).

Restatement Tier 1 DAMAGES

R3d Trusts Vol. 4 — Punitive Damages & Attorney Fees

Rule: Punitive damages available for egregious breach that is malicious, in bad faith, fraudulent, reckless, or self-serving. Attorney fees as make-whole: litigation costs recoverable; pro se effort has monetary value.

Application: LoanCare qualifies on ALL five counts: (1) Failed RESPA QWR timelines, (2) Confirmed Ghost Pool CD7957 then went silent, (3) Became insolvent while holding borrower funds, (4) Sub-serviced void chain with knowledge. Nature and extent of misconduct = maximum award.

Bluebook: Restatement (Third) of Trusts (Am. L. Inst. 2012).

TIER 3 — Defense Killers

Defense Killer

§ 5. Negligence Does Not Bar Claim

“A claimant does not bear the risk of a mistake merely because the mistake results from the claimant's negligence.” Family's failure to discover broken chain does NOT bar recovery.

Defense Killer

§ 65. Change of Position — UNAVAILABLE to Wrongdoers

Only available to innocent recipients. Conscious wrongdoers in a fraud scheme cannot invoke this defense.

Defense Killer

§ 66. Bona Fide Purchaser — Defeated

Void transfers (not voidable) = BFP irrelevant. Institutional defendants had constructive notice. Judicial lien creditors are NOT purchasers (§ 60).
